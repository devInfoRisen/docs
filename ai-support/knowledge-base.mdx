Your knowledge base is the collection of articles and FAQs that Nexa's AI reads before responding to a customer question. The more relevant and well-written your articles are, the more accurately the AI can answer without involving you. You control every article — add, edit, or remove them at any time from the Nexa dashboard, and the AI picks up changes immediately.

## Adding a Knowledge Base Article

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Knowledge Base">
    In the Nexa dashboard, navigate to **AI Support → Knowledge Base**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click Add Article">
    Select **Add Article** in the top-right corner of the Knowledge Base page.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter a Title and Content">
    Give the article a clear, descriptive title — ideally phrased as the question your customer would ask. Then write the answer in the content field.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tag with Relevant Topics">
    Add one or more topic tags (for example, `orders`, `refills`, `pricing`). Tags help the AI retrieve the right article faster and let you organize your knowledge base as it grows.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Publish">
    Click **Publish**. The article is immediately available to the AI. You can return at any time to edit or unpublish it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Tips for Writing Effective Articles

Good articles lead to accurate, helpful AI responses. Keep these guidelines in mind when writing:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Use a clear question as the title">
    Write the title exactly as a customer would ask it — for example, *"How long does delivery take?"* rather than *"Delivery times"*. This improves how well the AI matches incoming questions to the right article.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Keep answers concise and direct">
    Aim for short, actionable answers. Customers reading a bot reply want a fast resolution, not a wall of text. If a topic needs depth, split it into multiple focused articles.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Cover common objections and follow-up questions">
    Think about the follow-up your customers usually send after an initial answer. Address those points in the same article to reduce back-and-forth.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Avoid jargon your customers wouldn't use">
    Write in the same language your customers use when they message your bot. If they say "order not moving," use that phrase — not "service fulfillment delay."
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Tip>
  Seed your knowledge base with your top 10 most common support questions first. Even a small, high-quality knowledge base dramatically reduces your manual support volume from day one.
</Tip>

## Configuring Ticket Escalation

When the AI's confidence falls below a threshold you set, Nexa automatically creates a support ticket instead of sending a potentially wrong answer. Configure this threshold to match how much risk you're comfortable with.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open AI Support Settings">
    Go to **AI Support → Settings** in your Nexa dashboard.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the Confidence Threshold">
    Use the **Escalation Threshold** slider to choose the minimum confidence level required for the AI to reply on its own. A higher threshold means the AI escalates more often; a lower threshold means it answers more questions itself.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save Your Settings">
    Click **Save**. The new threshold applies to all incoming questions immediately.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Handling Escalated Tickets

When the AI escalates a conversation, the ticket appears in **Tickets** in your Nexa dashboard. To handle it:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Tickets Section">
    Navigate to **Tickets** in the Nexa sidebar. New tickets are marked with an **Open** status.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review the Conversation">
    Click a ticket to see the full conversation history, including the question the AI could not answer.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Reply to the Customer">
    Type your response in the reply field. Nexa sends it through the same channel (WhatsApp or Telegram) the customer originally used.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Close the Ticket">
    Once the issue is resolved, mark the ticket as **Closed**. Optionally, use the conversation as inspiration for a new knowledge base article to prevent the same escalation in the future.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Every escalated ticket is an opportunity to improve your knowledge base. If you see the same question escalated more than once, add an article for it — you'll stop seeing that ticket type entirely.
</Note>
